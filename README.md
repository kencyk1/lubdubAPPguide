# LubDubAPP Guide

An interactive guide for cardiovascular advanced practice provider (APP) training, from introductory anatomy to in-depth hemodynamics, rhythm and pacing, structural interventions and echocardiography: a beating 3D model of a real adult heart with the cardiac cycle, pressure tracings and heart sounds, valve disease graded from mild to severe with chamber remodeling, conduction blocks, pacing and arrhythmias with catheter ablation, structural interventions step by step (TAVI, edge-to-edge repair, transcatheter valve replacement, balloon valvuloplasty, septal and appendage closure), coronary territories with acute occlusion scenarios (regional wall motion and the 12-lead ECG), a simulated cine coronary angiogram in the standard projections, and simulated transthoracic (TTE) and transesophageal (TEE) echo views with colour and spectral Doppler.

> **For non-commercial education only.** This is a simulated teaching model. Pressures, velocities and measurements are typical adult values produced by the model, not patient data. Nothing here is medical advice or a substitute for clinical judgment, current guidelines or supervised training; do not use it for diagnosis or patient care.

**Further learning:** for ultrasound physics and in-depth echocardiography, continue with [The Heart by Ultrasound (intro2echo.com)](https://intro2echo.com/).

## Use it

- **Online:** <https://lubdubappguide.com/>
- **On a computer or a phone:** the teaching card for each scene stays beside the 3D view on a computer and sits just under it on a phone, so the text and the heart can be read together.

## What is in it

- **Anatomy:** rotate the heart, peel back the wall, and select any chamber, valve, vessel, coronary branch or conduction structure for its function and a clinical pearl. C-arm angles (LAO/RAO, CRA/CAU) update live as you rotate, with RAO, LAO, 3-cusp coplanar and surgeon's mitral views, plus four standard angiographic projections (spider, LAO cranial, RAO caudal, RAO cranial) in the coronary scenes. Besides the model's own coronary arteries, the smaller branches are drawn on its surface: second diagonal, further obtuse marginals, posterolateral, conus, SA and AV nodal, RV and inferior septal branches.
- **Coronary territories:** the LV seen from its apex with the 17 AHA segments coloured by the LAD, circumflex and RCA, a matching bull's-eye, the echo views that show each wall, the ECG leads that face it, and septal perforators drawn from the LAD into the septum.
- **Cine angiogram:** a simulated coronary angiogram of the left or right coronary artery. Contrast fills the branches from the catheter tip (mostly in diastole on the left) and washes out, over the beating heart with faint shadows of the heart, spine, ribs and diaphragm. Seven left and five right standard projections, each with what it shows best; a lead II strip, a small 3D heart at the same angle, and click-to-name vessels with their territory.
- **Cardiac cycle:** 70 bpm with Lead II ECG, phonocardiogram, and Ao, LV, LA, PCWP, PA, RV and RA pressures. Pause and drag the phase bar or any tracing to scrub through the beat. Filters show the left heart, the right heart, or LV and RV together (constriction versus restriction).
- **Scenarios:** MR, TR, AS and chronic AR on a mild-to-severe slider graded to ASE and ACC/AHA cut-offs; acute AR; MS in sinus rhythm and in AF; TS; obstructive HCM; restrictive cardiomyopathy; constrictive pericarditis. The chambers remodel with each disease (LVH, dilation, atrial and RV enlargement). Acute proximal LAD, circumflex and proximal RCA occlusion (with RV infarction) make their territory akinetic in the 3D model and on echo, raise filling pressures, and show a 12-lead ECG with ST elevation and reciprocal changes, plus right-sided V4R (RCA) or posterior V7–V9 (circumflex). Constrictive and restrictive physiology include the respiratory variation of mitral and tricuspid inflow.
- **Rhythm and pacing:** 26 scenarios on the same beating heart, with leads II and V1, a 12-lead ECG, heart sounds and every pressure driven by one rhythm generator. Conduction: sinus, first-degree AV block, Mobitz I and II, complete heart block with junctional or ventricular escape, RBBB and LBBB, with the level of block marked in 3D. Pacing: VVI (transvenous or leadless), pacemaker syndrome, DDD (transvenous or dual-chamber leadless), CRT on and off, and left bundle branch area pacing, with the leads, capsules and generator in place. Tachycardia: PACs and PVCs, AVNRT, WPW and orthodromic AVRT, typical flutter (2:1 and 4:1), AF, scar VT and outflow-tract VT, with the re-entry circuit or focus animated in 3D and an Ablate button that lays the lesion set (slow pathway, accessory pathway, cavotricuspid isthmus, pulmonary vein isolation, VT isthmus, RVOT focus) and converts the rhythm. Cannon a waves, S2 splitting (wide in RBBB, paradoxical in LBBB and RV pacing) and the blood-pressure cost of each rhythm follow from the timing.
- **Structural interventions:** nine procedures shown step by step on the beating heart: TAVI (balloon-expandable and self-expanding), mitral and tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair, transcatheter mitral and tricuspid valve replacement, balloon mitral valvuloplasty, ASD and PFO closure, perimembranous and post-infarct VSD closure, and left atrial appendage occlusion (single-lobe and lobe-and-disc). Wires, guides and delivery systems come up from the femoral vessels (or the jugular vein) along this heart's own IVC, aorta, fossa ovalis and valve axes; generic devices are crimped, steered, deployed and released. Each step carries the imaging that guides it and a pearl, and the pressures, sounds and numbers change from before to after (gradients, LA and RA pressures, Qp:Qs and oximetry). Key complications switch the hemodynamics and the 3D view: paravalvular leak, heart block, coronary obstruction, annular rupture with rapidly progressive tamponade, single-leaflet attachment, iatrogenic stenosis, LVOT obstruction, embolization, residual shunt, erosion, device thrombus and others.
- **Echo:** 23 TTE and TEE views with simulated B-mode, colour Doppler (flow convergence, PISA, aliasing), PW and CW Doppler with live measurements, end-diastolic calipers, a TEE omniplane angle indicator, a Doppler cursor that shows the beam and sample gate on the 2D image, and a territories overlay that colours the LV wall by coronary artery.
- **Sounds:** synthesized heart sounds in sync with the model, plus real patient recordings.

The conduction system, chordae, moderator band, septal perforator arteries, the smaller coronary branches (including the distal first diagonal and obtuse marginal), valve leaflets, left atrial appendage lumen and fossa ovalis are drawn by the app because the source model lacks them. Coronary territories follow the usual right-dominant distribution of the AHA 17-segment model, laid onto this heart using its own interventricular grooves; the 12-lead ECGs are model tracings built to show the typical patterns. Echo images, Doppler and pressures are simulated from the model. The cine angiogram is computed from the vessel centrelines with normal adult lumen sizes, X-ray attenuation by path length and simplified contrast flow; it is not a recording, and the standard angles are applied to this one heart. The rhythm scenarios are generated, not recorded: ECG complexes are built from simple wave shapes to show the textbook patterns, pacing spikes are drawn larger than real bipolar spikes, the pacing hardware, re-entry circuits, scar and ablation lesions are schematic, and breathing is not modelled in these scenarios. The structural-intervention devices are generic classes drawn by the app, not any manufacturer's design, and brand names appear only in the text; catheter routes are fitted to this one heart, whose left atrium is shallow, so the transseptal puncture sits lower above the mitral valve than the usual target. Pressures before and after each procedure blend the model's disease states, and the heart rate stays at 70 bpm in shock states.
