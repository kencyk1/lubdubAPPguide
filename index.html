LubDubAPP Guide

An interactive guide for cardiovascular advanced practice provider (APP) training, from introductory anatomy to in-depth hemodynamics and echocardiography. It is built on a segmented model of a real adult heart from the Visible Human dataset: rotate it, peel back the wall, and select any chamber, valve, vessel or coronary branch to see what it does and why it matters clinically. For ultrasound physics and in-depth echo learning, continue with The Heart by Ultrasound (intro2echo.com).

For non-commercial education only. A simulated teaching model, not medical advice; not for diagnosis or patient care.

For ultrasound physics and in-depth echocardiography, see The Heart by Ultrasound (intro2echo.com). A simulated B-mode image, formed from the beating 3D model's cross-section in standard display orientation: speckle that moves with the wall, lateral blur that grows with depth, bright echoes where an interface faces the beam, the pericardium, chest-wall or liver near field, and acoustic shadowing behind a calcified valve. Valve leaflets are drawn on the model's annulus and open and close from the pressure model (the mitral and tricuspid leaflets also follow inflow: wide open in the E wave, drifting closed in diastasis, reopening with the A wave); spectral Doppler comes from the same pressures with v = √(ΔP/4). Colour Doppler is a velocity field read along each beam line: flow converging on a regurgitant or stenotic orifice (its aliasing shell is the PISA), a jet that slows and widens downstream, aliasing past the Nyquist limit and a mosaic in fast jets; the orifice follows the severity setting. The chambers remodel with each disease (LVH, dilation, atrial and RV enlargement) and the calipers measure the cut at end-diastole. The model's appendage is a collapsed lobe and its septum a uniform band, so the left atrial appendage lumen (with pectinate muscles) and the fossa ovalis are drawn by the simulator. On the traces

    At the bedside

      Motion is a smooth deformation of the static model; the LV wall thickens as it contracts, and in a coronary occlusion the shaded territory neither moves inward nor thickens. Valves turn green while open, amber while regurgitant and orange while open but obstructed; the cone in 3D marks the abnormal jet. Pressures are illustrative composites built so every valve event falls where the pressures cross, then played out beat by beat (AF, PVCs, respiration) — teaching curves, not patient recordings. Model sounds are synthesized from the same timeline; the patient recordings are real and come from the open BMD-HS dataset.

      3D model: Human Reference Atlas, CCF 3D Reference Object Library (HuBMAP) — Visible Human Male heart and heart vasculature, v1.2. Licensed CC BY 4.0; merged, simplified and recolored here for teaching. The vessel the source model names “diagonal branch of the LAD” runs from the left main in the left AV groove and gives off the obtuse marginal, so it is labeled here as the circumflex. The conduction system, chordae tendineae, moderator band and septal perforator arteries are not part of the source model; they are drawn schematically, fitted to the model’s own landmarks (the SVC–RA junction, Koch’s triangle, the septal crest beneath the aortic valve, the septal surfaces, the papillary muscles and the leaflets). Echo images are simulated from the beating model’s cross-section in each view’s plane: B-mode speckle and walls move with the beat, valve leaflets open and close from the pressure model, and colour and spectral Doppler are calculated from the same pressures (simplified Bernoulli, v = √(ΔP/4); flow convergence and aliasing at the Nyquist limit). The left atrial appendage lumen and the fossa ovalis are drawn by the simulator because the source model lacks them. Coronary territories follow the usual right-dominant distribution of the AHA 17-segment model, laid onto this LV using its own interventricular grooves; in the occlusion scenarios the territory stops moving inward and thickening, and the 12-lead ECG is a model tracing built to show the typical pattern. Probe positions, planes and the omniplane angles shown are fitted to this one heart, so depths and angles are approximate; C-arm angles are read from the 3D camera. Pressures and percentages are typical adult values; not for diagnosis.
